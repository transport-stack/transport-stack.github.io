‘India Urban Mobility Forum’: A Roundtable series
Roundup of Roundtable 2 on “Intelligent Transit at Scale: AI, Agentic Systems and the Digital Foundations of Urban Mobility” · Organized on 21st October 2026
RT-2 — panellists and participants at the close of the session.
Roundtable 2 brought together transit agency leaders, government officials, JICA, academics and startups — 75 participants in all — around one question: how do we go from pilots to AI-ready transport agencies at population scale? The discussion moved from what agencies are already achieving with their own data, to why sharing it across agencies is still so hard.
The panel: Atsushi Yamanaka (JICA), Sanjay Biswal (Ex-MD CRUT, Ex-GM OSRTC), Gokul T G (KMRL), Manish Srivastava (ARF) and Udit Bhatia (IIT Gandhinagar & AIReSQ), moderated by Prof. Pravesh Biyani (IIIT-Delhi).
Key outcomes of the Roundtable
• Consensus: Agencies that put their own data to work already see results — better scheduling, fewer cancellations and lower costs in Odisha; clear visibility into bus operations in Kochi
• Barriers: Sharing data is a governance problem, not a technology one — data is guarded as an asset, locked behind vendor paywalls, and there is no clear open data policy for real-time feeds
• What’s needed: An open data policy that settles access and costs, shared state-wide infrastructure, and use cases that show value in rupees saved and services improved
From the ground: what data is already doing
What’s working
• Disaggregated ticketing data: In Odisha, electronic ticketing machines record trips by women, students and senior citizens — guiding route optimisation, bus-size allocation and dynamic scheduling
• Depot & fuel management: Pre-departure vehicle checks cut daily trip cancellations from ~100–120 to under 20, and fuel monitoring delivered large diesel savings — helping OSRTC turn a loss into a profit in FY 2025-26
• Owning the data: Kochi wrote data ownership into its bus tender, then combined telematics, charging and fare data to see how the whole system performs — the metro and water metro now plan to follow
What’s holding it back
• Data kept, not exchanged: Data only creates value when it is exchanged, yet agencies treat it as something to guard rather than share
• Paywalls and firewalls: Even when a city generates the data, it often sits with private vendors who charge for access — so pilots stall before their value can be shown
• Finance-first scrutiny: Finance teams ask for server costs and returns before funding real-time feeds like GTFS-RT; network-wide benefits are hard to price
• No real-time data policy: No clear guidance on whether real-time access should be free or metered, leaving agencies unsure how to budget for it
Where AI fits
Garbage in, garbage out
AI is only as good as its data. Open data infrastructure is the foundation — and AI can in turn help clean data and make it AI-ready
Scale is the trigger
Small fleets can run on analytics; AI becomes essential as data grows and is shared across agencies
Demand-responsive operations
Moving feeder buses from fixed headways to demand-based deployment, timed to metro and water metro arrivals
Humans in the loop
Agencies want AI support for daily operational decisions — with clear limits and human oversight
What the audience said
• Most valuable use cases in the next two years: Real-time passenger information & tracking (81%), demand forecasting & planning (62%), AI that helps passenger journeys (58%)
• Biggest barriers to AI in transit: Data quality & access (81%), vendor barriers (44%), skills & staffing (44%)
Live polls during the session; respondents could pick more than one option.
Call to action: How to move ahead?
As in Roundtable 1, the path forward runs on two levels — policy to set the rules of the road, and agency practice to prove the value.
Policy level
• Open data policy for real-time feeds: A detailed policy that settles access, costs and fee structure, so agencies can budget for GTFS-RT with confidence
• Shared state-wide infrastructure: Roll out GTFS and GTFS-RT across all cities of a state in one go — big enough for officials to commit to, and deliverable within a year
• Govern data as a shared resource: Clear rules on who controls access, manages schemas and captures the value created
Agency level
• Own your data in contracts: Write data ownership and access into tenders, as Kochi did for its bus telematics
• Start where it hurts most: Pick use cases tied to everyday pain points and show the gains in rupees and service quality
• Reward change: Recognise departments that adopt open systems — visible results and peer recognition drive culture change

What’s on Transport Stack
Transport Stack Hackathon 2026
Announced at Roundtable 2, the Transport Stack Hackathon invites builders to turn mobility ideas into working, open-source prototypes using real Delhi transit data — live bus GPS, e-ticketing, ETM and metro AFC records.
Transport Stack Hackathon 2026 — register by 20 November.
Who can join
Students, startups, researchers and developers — anyone who can build with data
Prizes
₹2,75,000 in prizes; standout solutions move toward open-source release on Transport Stack
How it works
Phase 1 (open to all): a proposal and working prototype for Problem 1 or 2. Phase 2 (shortlisted teams): refine, or take on Problems 3–5, with weekly mentoring
Grand finale
11 December 2026 at IIIT-Delhi — solutions are presented and judged at the Transport Stack annual event
Five problem statements
Each problem comes with a full brief covering background, data and success criteria:
1 · Bus travel-time planner
A Google Maps-like API that estimates bus travel times between any two points
2 · Crowdedness-aware planner
Journey options ranked by both travel time and expected crowding
3 · Public transit OD matrix
Where trips start and end, scaled up from e-ticketing and metro gate data
4 · Metro feeder bus routes
Feeder routes for ~500 e-buses that respect road, range and charging limits
5 · Metro & bus crowding
Load by line, segment and time of day from fare-collection data

Data, constraints and judging criteria for every problem. Read the briefs

Innovation Spotlight
Roundtable 2 closed with a startup showcase — today’s feature is MobiReSQ by AIReSQ, a startup founded by Udit Bhatia (IIT Gandhinagar) that helps cities keep moving when extreme rain floods their streets.
AIReSQ’s pipeline — from city data and physics-guided AI to flood-aware mobility decisions.
• Physics-guided AI: Physics models and AI emulators turn rainfall forecasts into city-scale flood maps, fast
• Morning flood outlook: Every day at 8 AM, a forecast of the city’s flood footprint and the hotspots expected by evening
• Flood-aware routing: Finds the road closures that would trigger the worst cascading jams, and reroutes public, private and emergency vehicles
• Live road status: Which roads are closed or jammed right now, and how much extra travel time flooding is adding — at hyperlocal scale
• In use today: Municipal Corporation of Gurugram, as part of its traffic data stack, and the Roads & Buildings Department, Government of Gujarat
