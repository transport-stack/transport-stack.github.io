'India Urban Mobility Forum': A Roundtable series
 
Roundup of Roundtable 1 on "Open Data & Interoperability: Unlocking Urban Mobility Innovation" · Organized on 12th June 2026
RT-1 — the India Mobility Forum roundtable in session.
Roundtable 1 convened transit planners, academics, operators and builders to discuss opening up India's public-transport data. The conversation covered what's working, what's broken, and what needs to happen next — from vendor lock-in and data quality to procurement reform and national policy.
Key outcomes of the Roundtable
• Consensus: Open data and open systems are essential for public transport in India — proven in Delhi and via Namma Yatri
• Barriers: Data locked in proprietary vendor systems, unwillingness to share, poor data quality, weak institutional capacity
• What's needed: National policy mandates, capacity building for agencies, standardized procurement, open-source platform adoption
Current state of open mobility systems
What's working
Success demonstrated with open data: Larger ecosystem drives innovation - startups / researchers build for citizens, operators, planners. E.g., strong solutions from startups in Smart Cities Mission's Transport for All Challenge, even smaller cities like Belgavi, Davangere
Impact showcased: Open data creates value across public & private players. E.g., Namma Yatri open-sourced its code & published non-personal business data, enabling academic research & city planning in Chennai and Bengaluru
Government engagement: Growing engagement from government agencies - Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) supported Smart City Mission
  
What's not working
Data locked in vendor systems: Challenges of non-streamlined vendor contracts and vendor lock-in; data often locked in vendor proprietary systems with no easy path to open it up
Unwillingness to share: Agencies resist sharing because open data has the potential to expose service gaps and coverage shortfalls
Poor data quality: Quality of shared data is typically poor and ~ 60% of project time is spent cleaning and fixing it before it can be used
Officials' churn: Frequent churn of PTO leadership leads to difficulties in driving long term thinking / programs, with institutional memory lost at each transition
Why National Scale-up stalls
India's diversity
Every state/city is different — reaching each SDU (State/Sub-national Delivery Unit) takes far longer.
  
No central mandate
Unlike roads/flyovers, open data has no enforcement as law/rule.
 
Capacity gaps
Agencies can't code or maintain data; each reinvents the wheel.
  
Broken procurement
Tendering blocks innovation; marketplace-based procurement is needed.
Open questions
• Joint ownership: Should data generated on public systems be jointly owned with the operator?
• Vendor continuity: How do we balance open systems with vendor lock-in?
• Revenue Sharing: How do we compensate individuals whose aggregated data creates value?
Call to action: How to move ahead?
The forward path is split across two levels — national alignment to set the direction, and institutional capability to make it stick.
National level
Central alignment: If Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) sets standards, everyone follows — central alignment is the single fastest path to national scale across states and cities
Data standards: Define and mandate common data standards so every agency publishes to the same baseline, making datasets comparable and interoperable across the country
  
Institutional level
Dedicated in-house Tech teams: Build in-house Tech capability & skill within PTO organizations to drive digital transformation programs & ensure continuity, reduce vendor dependence
Augment Public Procurement & Tender process: Standardize specifications in documents to drive simplification and help homogenize data sets across cities

What's on Transport Stack
 
Delhi Transport Stack
Delhi Transport Stack is Delhi's live, multimodal open-data platform. This is where real city data is published, standardized and served today.
Multimodal coverage
Buses, metros and shared mobility — all standardized in one place and served through a single, consistent interface
  
Drive innovation
Transparent ecosystem designed to foster mobility innovation & remove barriers for innovators
 
Open Transit Data migration in progress
Older Open Transit Data (OTD) platform being deprecated; all data moving to Transport Stack
  
Ticketing & ridership
Datasets in future pipeline, to be released post-approval from the relevant authorities
Representative datasets on the platform
A snapshot of what's published today — the list keeps growing as more modes come online:
Bus static data (GTFS)
Routes, stops, schedules and trip definitions for Delhi's bus network
  
Bus real-time data (AVLS)
Live vehicle positions from on-board GPS units
  
Bus ETA & derived feeds
Arrival predictions and bus-bunching detection from raw AVLS
 
Metro data
Metro routes, stations and schedules in the same standardized format
  
Intermediate Public Transport
Shared-mobility data around metro stations
  
Journey planner APIs
Public API endpoints for multimodal trip planning
Master Transport Stack
Master Transport Stack is the open-source core — the GitHub repository and Wiki — that any transport organization can adopt and scale. Delhi Transport Stack is the working base; the Master Transport Stack is the reusable platform above it.
Open-source code base
Journey planner, ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival) services, bus-bunching detection — all public repos
  
Built to scale
Designed so any transport organization can set up its own stack, not just Delhi
 
GitHub repository
The shared codebase — browse, fork, file issues and contribute
  
Wiki & documentation
How-tos, data dictionaries and architectural notes

Innovation Spotlight
 
The success of an open data platform is demonstrated by the innovation built on top of it — today's feature focuses on the Talking Box — a voice assistant for bus stops, created by the Chartr team & live in Delhi.
Talking Box — two form factors for different stop types.
• Press. Talk. Done: One-button interaction; no smartphone needed
• Multilingual: Hindi & English today, more languages coming
• Real-time ETA: Live arrivals, journey planning and route guidance spoken aloud
• Static fallback: Uses scheduled APIs when real-time buses are unavailable
• Powered by live GTFS + AVLS data: Flowing through Delhi Transport Stack
Product page: chartr.in/talking-box
