Roundtable-1 Roundup

India Urban Mobility Forum series · Roundtable 1 — Open Data & Interoperability · Organised on 12th June 2026

RT-1 — the India Mobility Forum roundtable in session.

Roundtable-1 convened transit planners, academics, operators and builders to discuss opening up India's public-transport data. The conversation covered what's working, what's broken, and what needs to happen next — from vendor lock-in and data quality to procurement reform and national policy.

Key outcomes

Consensus: open data and open systems are essential for public transport in India — proven in Delhi and via Namma Yatri.

Barriers: data locked in proprietary vendor systems, unwillingness to share, poor data quality (60% of project time), weak institutional capacity.

What's needed: national policy mandates, capacity building for agencies, standardized procurement, open-source platform adoption.

Where the discussion converged

No formal votes were taken — what follows is where the room's discussion converged rather than binding decisions.

Public ownership of transit data

Taxpayer-funded data should be publicly owned — monetization models can still exist on top.

Open systems over proprietary

Build on open-source platforms to avoid vendor lock-in and enable third-party innovation.

Data exchange architecture

Use data exchanges as an intermediate layer to separate data from applications.

Current state of open mobility systems

What's working

Platforms exist: IUDX and Transport Stack are built and proven functional.
Expertise available: IISc, IIIT Delhi, IIT Kanpur — on both technology and policy.
Success cases: Transport for All challenge — startups built solid solutions on limited data.
Government interest: MoHUA and Smart City Mission engaged.

What's not working

Data locked in vendor systems: no incentive to open up; takes years to force.
Unwillingness to share: cities fear exposing service gaps.
Poor data quality: 60% of project time fixing it; 37% of trips break route IDs.
No data SLAs: feeds shut off arbitrarily despite heavy citizen use.
Privacy paralysis: DPDP uncertainty leads to inaction.
Operators seen as competitors: cities withhold bus stop locations.
Official churn: frequent transfers erase institutional memory.

Data ownership and legal framework

Ownership clarity

Transit data is a public good: taxpayer-funded data should be taxpayer-owned.
Namma Yatri model: a private entity opened its data for trust, enabling research in Chennai and Bangalore.

Legal and policy context

Personal data protected: DPDP Act (mandatory 2027) needs consent for PII.
Non-personal data unregulated: no ownership concept — a "free for all" with no sharing mandate.
Geospatial exception: National Geospatial Policy (2022) mandates publicly-funded geospatial data be public.
No near-term change: non-personal framework unlikely to be revisited for a decade after the 6–8 year DPDP process.

Scaling challenges and solutions

Why national scale-up fails

India's diversity Every state/city is different — reaching each SDU takes far longer. No central mandate Unlike roads/flyovers, open data has no enforcement as law/rule.
Capacity gaps Agencies can't code or maintain data; each reinvents the wheel. Broken procurement Tendering blocks innovation; marketplace-based procurement is needed.

Proposed solutions

National level

Central alignment: if MoHUA sets standards, everyone follows — fastest path to scale.
Policy → law: move from guidance to enforceable acts/rules.
ONDC model: 9+ metros and bus entities joining.

Institutional level

Capacity programs: standard formats for crew, bus, depot management.
Handhold agencies: prove value via use cases like Chennai tax overlays.
Tender specs: mandatory clauses for data ownership.

Technical level

Operational data standards: India-specific crew/fleet/depot standards.
Software escrow: UK-style model for continuity.
Open-source community: vendors build on a shared base.

Data quality and standards

Quality issues identified

Route ID problems: 37% of trips don't follow route IDs.
Research impact: quality limits research; much time goes to quantifying it.
Metrics needed: platform should publish quality metrics alongside the data.

Standardization gaps

AIS-140 GPS standards: present at policy level, implementation varies.
ASRTU rate contracts: vendors chase e-ticketing; apps undelivered.
GTFS insufficient: needs an operational data layer beneath it.

Open questions

Revenue sharing: how do we compensate individuals whose aggregated data creates value?
Joint ownership: should data generated on public systems be jointly owned with the operator?
Vendor continuity: how do we balance open systems with vendor lock-in?

What we're taking forward

1
 Research consortium Prof. Gita, Prof. Pravesh and Prof. Inder to build India-specific operational data standards.
2
 Capacity-building curriculum Train transit agencies on data-coding standards and maintenance.
3
 Standard tender template Mandatory clauses ensuring data ownership for all SDU procurements.
4
 Data-quality metrics Record and publish quality metrics on the Delhi Transport Stack platform.

What's on Transport Stack

A look at the platform's features, the data already published, and what's being built on top of it.

Platform features

Multimodal coverageBuses, metros and shared mobility — all standardized in one place. Open-source code baseJourney planner, ETA services, bus-bunching detection — all public repos.
Low entry barrierTransparent ecosystem designed to cut the cost of entry. OTD migration in progressOlder Open Transit Data platform being deprecated; all data moving to Transport Stack.

Representative datasets on the platform

A snapshot of what's published today — the list keeps growing as more modes and cities come online:

Bus static data (GTFS)Routes, stops, schedules and trip definitions for Delhi's bus network. Bus real-time data (AVLS)Live vehicle positions from on-board GPS units. Bus ETA & derived feedsArrival predictions and bus-bunching detection from raw AVLS.
Metro dataMetro routes, stations and schedules in the same standardized format. Intermediate Public TransportShared-mobility data around metro stations. Journey planner APIsPublic API endpoints for multimodal trip planning.
Coming soon: ticketing & ridership. Delhi's ticketing data exists but sits in a disguised internal system; transport-department approval is pending before it can be released.

Built on the stack

The point of an open data platform is what people build on top of it. Here's a featured build — with space for many more.

Talking Box — two form factors for different stop types.

Talking Box — voice assistant for bus stops

Built by Chartr · live in Delhi

Press. Talk. Done. — one-button interaction; no smartphone needed.
Multilingual: Hindi & English today, more languages coming.
Real-time ETA: live arrivals, journey planning and route guidance spoken aloud.
Static fallback: uses scheduled APIs when real-time buses are unavailable.
Powered by Chartr's live GTFS + AVLS data flowing through Transport Stack.
