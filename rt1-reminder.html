What is the Transport Stack, and why should you care?

Transport Stack is an open platform of software and data for Indian urban mobility. Think GTFS datasets, ETA calculators, transit tools, open transit data APIs, a Wiki, and a collection of GitHub repositories with working code.

If you've ever wished someone would just publish the bus stops in your city in a machine-readable way, or build a tool that flags when schedules drift – that's literally what this is. Roundtable 1 is the open-floor conversation for the people building it.

What is GTFS, and why does every transit app secretly use it?

GTFS – General Transit Feed Specification – is a widely-accepted, CSV-based format that lets a city bus agency publish their schedules and stop locations in a way that any app (Google Maps, Moovit, Here maps, Uber, your city's startup) can ingest. It's the quiet connective tissue that makes "see the next bus" work in a hundred cities worldwide.

India has the most buses in the world, but GTFS coverage is patchy. Making a city's network machine-readable is unglamorous, slow, and political – but it's the bottleneck behind most of transit "data cleaning" costs. The roundtable will look at what it actually takes to open GTFS datasets, from researchers, operators, and think tanks who've done it.

Do you know how ONDC actually works?

Most people hear "ONDC" and think "UPI for everything." The reality is closer to a Beckn-flavoured control tower that lets any buyer app talk to any seller app, without either of them having to do a custom integration. From ride-hailing apps to the part of ONDC where you can buy from local kirana stores on your regular payments app, everything runs on top of it.

The Roundtable 1 panel includes someone who has been on the inside of building one of the highest-volume ONDC mobility networks in India. If you want to know what ONDC actually takes on the ground – not in slideware – bring your questions.
